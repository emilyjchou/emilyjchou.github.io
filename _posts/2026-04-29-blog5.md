### HCI Blog Series (5/6) – Idea to Hi-Fidelity Prototype

# From Idea to Hi-Fidelity Prototype: Designing with Purpose

Designing isn’t just about making something look good—it’s about making something *work well for people*. This phase of my project pushed me to move beyond aesthetics and really think about usability, clarity, and user experience.

In this blog, I’ll walk through how I evaluated my medium-fidelity prototype, refined my design, and transformed it into a polished hi-fidelity prototype.

---

## Evaluating My UI with Heuristic Evaluation

To critically analyze my design, I used **Nielsen’s 10 Usability Heuristics**, which are widely used principles for evaluating user interfaces. These heuristics helped me step into the user’s shoes and identify friction points.

### My Approach

I went through each screen of my prototype and asked:
- Is the system status visible?
- Are interactions intuitive?
- Can users recover from errors easily?

This structured approach helped me uncover issues I hadn’t noticed before.

### Most Challenging Heuristic

The hardest heuristic for me was:

**“Consistency and Standards”**

I realized that:
- Some buttons looked different across screens
- Navigation patterns weren’t always predictable

Even small inconsistencies made the experience feel less intuitive.

### Usability Issues I Found

Some key problems included:
- Confusing navigation flow between pages
- Lack of feedback after user actions
- Overcrowded sections that overwhelmed users

One surprising discovery was how something that looked “clean” to me actually felt unclear to a user.

### Impact of Evaluation

This process helped me:
- Simplify interactions
- Improve clarity
- Make the interface more intuitive

It shifted my mindset from *“Does this look good?”* to *“Does this make sense to the user?”*

---

## Refining My Design

After identifying usability issues, I focused on improving both functionality and visual design.

### Key Changes I Made

- Simplified navigation structure  
- Standardized buttons, colors, and typography  
- Added feedback (e.g., confirmations, hover states)  
- Reduced clutter and improved spacing  

### Aligning with My Original Goals

My original goal was to create a **simple, intuitive, and user-friendly experience**.  
After refinement, my design better aligned with this vision.

### Balancing Aesthetics and Usability

One of the biggest challenges was balancing:
- **Aesthetics** → clean visuals, colors, typography  
- **Usability** → clarity, accessibility, efficiency  

I learned that:
> A visually appealing design means nothing if users don’t understand how to use it.

## What I Learned from This Process

This stage taught me that design is **iterative, not linear**.

### Key Takeaways

- Users don’t think the way designers do  
- Small usability issues can create big frustrations  
- Feedback is essential for improvement  

### Rethinking My Design Decisions

The evaluation forced me to reconsider:
- My layout choices  
- How users move through the app  
- Whether my design truly supports user goals  

### Unexpected Insights

One major realization:
> Good UX is invisible—users shouldn’t have to think about how to use your product.

---

## How This Shapes My Future as a Designer

This experience changed how I view digital product design.

### What I’ll Carry Forward

- Always test designs early and often  
- Prioritize usability over personal preference  
- Design with empathy for the user  

### Applying This in My Career

In the future, I will:
- Use heuristic evaluation as a standard step  
- Focus on user-centered design  
- Continuously iterate based on feedback  

### Final Reflection

This project showed me that great design isn’t about perfection—it’s about **constant improvement**.
